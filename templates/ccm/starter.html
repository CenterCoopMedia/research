What connects a community?

Local news starts with people. This sample report follows the connections that turn one newsroom's reporting into shared public knowledge.

What to keep

Keep these three points.

  • One newsroom reaches the people it already serves. Two newsrooms that ask one question reach more of them.
  • A shared question needs one editor who holds the schedule and one place to put the work.
  • Every number in this sample is invented. Replace each one with a result you can source.

In brief

This page is the sample report of the CCM report template. It shows each part of a report in the order a reader meets it: the points to keep, the argument, a stage that changes as you scroll, a figure, a table, the results, a chronology, the terms, the method and the sources. The subject is invented. Use the page to judge the shape of a report, not the evidence in it.

The question

A story is only the beginning.

A reporter asks a question. A resident shares what they know. An editor connects the account to a wider story. Each exchange gives information a place to go.

A newsroom that works alone answers the question for its own readers. A newsroom that shares the question answers it for a region. That is collaborative reporting, and it is the subject of this sample.1

Follow the connections

From one newsroom to a shared network.

1 of 4

Start with the people doing the work.

Each mark stands for one imagined newsroom. At first, the marks sit apart. Each newsroom has its own sources, routines, and readers.

12 imagined newsrooms, working independently

2 of 4

Make room for a shared question.

A common reporting question brings newsrooms into small groups. Partners can compare records, share translation, and identify gaps in coverage.

Three groups, each convened by one newsroom

3 of 4

Build connections that last.

As partners exchange work, individual projects can become a network. The lines show possible relationships, not measured information flows.

A connected network of 12 imagined newsrooms

4 of 4

Keep the community at the center.

The shared work matters when people can find and use it. A report should explain who benefits, what changes, and what the evidence cannot tell us.

What comes next

Leave the reader with a clear next step.

Explain what the results mean for the people who can act on them. Connect each recommendation to evidence in the report, and say who has to do the work.

A group that shares a question can also share the parts that cost the most. A shared beat spreads the cost of one reporter. A news commons gives the group one place to plan. An agreement for content sharing puts the finished work in front of more readers.

Use the method note to say how you gathered the evidence. Name the limits, so a reader can judge what the report supports and what it does not.

Terms used in this report

collaborative reporting
Two or more newsrooms that report one story together. Each one publishes the work in its own voice.
content sharing
An agreement that lets a partner republish another partner's story, with credit, at no cost.
news commons
A network that gives the newsrooms of one region a place to plan, to train and to share what they make.
shared beat
One reporter who covers a subject for several newsrooms at once. The newsrooms share the cost and publish the same reporting.
